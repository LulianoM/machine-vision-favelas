## Materials and Methods

### 4.1 Research design, ethics and positionality

This is an experimental computational study in 2 parts. The first characterises the aerial target without any detector, measuring on generated corpora and on the public benchmark the size, shape and contrast of a person as functions of GSD, camera pitch and illumination (Section 4.4). The second trains detectors on synthetic imagery alone and evaluates them against a ceiling trained on real images where a public benchmark exists, and on held-out synthetic frames where none does (Sections 4.5 to 4.7). Synthetic data is a simulation whose verification against reality is confined to the benchmark.

Constraints fixed before any corpus was generated bound the study. No imagery of residents or of the territory is collected, and none is used in any result reported here: no imagery, geometry or address-level coordinate of Jacarezinho enters the generator, the corpora or any measurement. No field data of this study exists (Section 3.2). The only real data are two public benchmarks (Section 4.3). Any future field data would require an independent ethics review and a community engagement process external to the police institution before any deployment is considered. Synthesis is the only procedure we identified that yields a labelled corpus of the required morphology without imagery of residents who cannot meaningfully consent under the conditions of Section 3; it trades the consent problem for the transfer problem.

The authors collected no data in Jacarezinho, and the contextual description of Section 3 rests on public journalistic, human-rights, institutional and academic sources alone. The study is written from outside the territory and takes a security application as its object, a framing that foregrounds what an aerial system would detect over daily life, informal economies or how the community makes sense of its own space; the perspective of the residents such a system would observe is absent from it. Everything produced here is therefore treated as a measurement on synthetic imagery, never as a validated operational tool. The capability measured, telling an armed figure from an unarmed one in an aerial frame, is a dual-use capability: whatever it is worth bears on the decision to use lethal force in an operation of the kind described in Section 3. The study mitigates that risk by publishing the conditions under which the capability was not demonstrated, by releasing neither the generator nor the corpora nor the trained weights, by publishing no address-level coordinate, and by conditioning any use over the territory on an independent ethics review and on a process with the residents (Section 6.6). Conflicts of interest and data availability are stated in the Declarations.

### 4.2 Synthetic scene generator

Scenes are generated procedurally in Blender 5.2 (Blender Foundation, 2026), headless with a deterministic seed, and rendered with its Cycles path tracer on the GPU. No commercial asset is used: materials and the 4 tree models are public domain (Poly Haven, n.d.; CC0), the human figures are built from the CC0 assets of MakeHuman through MPFB, a MakeHuman derivative for Blender (MakeHuman Community, 2026; Section 4.2.2), and all other geometry is authored by the generator. The corpus is free of any imagery or geometry of the territory, so it sits by construction on the generic side of the ablation of Liao et al. (2026) (Section 2.2), a cost that Section 4.1 prevents us from measuring.

#### 4.2.1 Settlement layout

Layout is produced in 3 stages, each addressing a way in which naive procedural generation betrays itself from above (Figure @fig{fig1_nadir}; Figure @fig{fig3_beco} shows the same scene at street level).

Parcelling. The built core is subdivided by recursive binary partition. Each split receives a gap whose modal value is zero, so that most dwellings abut: zero with probability 0.82, 0.5 to 1.2 m with probability 0.12, 1.3 to 2.0 m with probability 0.052, and 2.3 to 3.1 m with probability 0.008. The second band is the alley, or beco, that defines the morphology; each lot is shrunk by 0.15 m and rotated about its centroid, so a zero gap leaves a seam rather than a shared wall.

Orientation. A single partition leaves straight runs of 10 or 20 dwellings, the strongest signature of procedural generation in a nadir view. The core is therefore divided by a Voronoi diagram into sub-districts with independent orientations (9 in the settlement of S-nadir-150, the corpus of record, and 6 to 12 in the other 5), each lot is rotated by up to 0.17 radians and every corner passes through a smooth domain warp.

Vertical accretion. In most dwellings of 2 or more storeys the upper floor is smaller than the one below and offset to one boundary, producing the L-shaped footprint that characterises the texture from above. Storey count is drawn around a low-frequency noise field with a per-dwelling scatter of 0.95 storeys (standard deviation, SD), so that taller and shorter zones form. Roofs carry water tanks, clotheslines, stair housings, satellite dishes, roofing sheets, rubble and exposed reinforcement bar, and cabling spans between rooftop anchors: the intended main source of local clutter, although no measurement isolates their share. The generator records the footprint, storey count and roof height of every structure.

Six settlements exist (Figure @fig{fig6_assentamentos}), differing in relief, forest cover, a corridor through the core, the palette of roofs and facades, the extent of the core, the storey distribution and the share of vacant lots; settlement 1 is the one of every single-settlement corpus.

#### 4.2.2 Human figures, weapons and confusable objects

Each rendering run builds a pool of 6 rigged, clothed meshes with MPFB and places copies of them. For each body MPFB draws sex, continuous age, musculature, weight, height, proportions, one of 23 skin textures and one of 10 hairstyles; each body receives one of 12 complete outfits. Per instance the generator varies the hue, saturation and value of the outfit and draws a scale between 0.92 and 1.08, a heading and a pose, with noise per bone; unarmed figures stand (0.36), walk (0.38), sit (0.14) or cross their arms (0.12), and 35 per cent of male instances are shirtless. Figures are placed one at a time, 45 per cent on rooftop slabs (75 per cent in the 35 m corpus, its twins and their held-out frames) and the rest in alley cells left free by an occupancy map computed from the footprints; 10 to 34 are drawn per frame, by corpus (Section 4.3).

Three classes are annotated: armed (carrying a firearm), unarmed carrying nothing, and unarmed carrying an elongated object of the kind carried in this territory. The third answers the objection of Section 2.3, that a weapon which is always the same asset lets a detector separate the classes on the asset library. Five firearms are built procedurally from boxes and cylinders: 2 rifles (recorded lengths 0.92 and 0.94 m), a carbine (0.62 m), a shotgun (1.05 m) and a pistol (0.20 m); the meshes of the rifles and the shotgun are longer than their recorded lengths. Against them stand 9 objects spanning 0.88 to 2.5 m: brooms, floor squeegees, plastic pipe, reinforcement bar, planks, closed umbrellas, hoes, rolled mats and hose. Both draw from one palette of 11 materials with fixed colour and roughness: metal, worn metal, wood, light wood and polymer occur on both kinds, so that dark and metallic surfaces alone cannot separate the classes; PVC, grey PVC, rust, dark cloth, bristle and coloured plastic occur only on objects. Both are carried through the same distribution of poses, held forward (0.42), at the side (0.26), slung on the back (0.16) or while walking (0.16). The item is attached at the right wrist (at the spine when slung) with an orientation fixed per pose rather than solved against the hands, so it does not always sit in the grip. An object whose recorded length exceeds 1.15 m, which no weapon's does, is re-angled in every pose to rest on the shoulder or point to the ground. A detector may therefore separate the classes by length, by the materials only objects have, or by carrying angle, a rule of the generator rather than an observation of how residents carry things; the carried-as-weapon test of Experiment C neutralises the third, on the original 75 m corpus only, and no test neutralises the first two. The 35 m corpus and the corpora derived from it draw the object with a more realistic distribution (forward 0.08, side 0.34, back 0.28, walking 0.30), the weapon keeping the distribution above, so that in those corpora the carrying pose is a fourth cue, which no test neutralises.

Two corpora predate this library. The pilot corpus and the corpus of record carry an earlier one: a rifle in 3 silhouettes, a pistol in 15 per cent of armed figures, every weapon recorded at 0.9 m, and the forward pose drawn with probability 0.46. Every later corpus, S-oblique included, carries the 5 models above. Two further firearms, a rifle of 1.10 m and a compact submachine gun of 0.55 m, enter no training corpus and serve only the unseen-weapon test.

#### 4.2.3 Illumination regimes

Rather than jittering one sun parameter, the generator defines 6 coherent lighting budgets (Figure @fig{fig2_climas}), each fixing together solar irradiance and colour, the angular diameter of the solar disc, a band of solar elevation, sky fill, scattering, aerosols and an exposure offset. They are hard sun (elevation 46 to 74 degrees, disc 0.6 degrees), low sun (9 to 25, 0.9), overcast (30 to 70, 24), heavy overcast (28 to 66, 38), haze (22 to 52, 7) and rain (24 to 60, 32). The disc diameter separates them physically: at 0.6 degrees the shadow edge is hard, and above roughly 20 degrees the cast shadow effectively disappears, as under a covered sky. Rain is a dark, diffuse budget with dense haze; no raindrop or wet surface is rendered. Each corpus sweeps the 6 regimes in order rather than sampling them, so that a small corpus stays balanced, and the regime of every image is recorded.

#### 4.2.4 Camera model and acquisition geometries

The virtual camera reproduces a commercially available UAV payload at its wide setting: a 4.3 mm lens on a 5.37 mm sensor, implemented as 28.8 mm on a 36 mm sensor with horizontal fit, a horizontal field of view of 63.96 degrees at 16:9 and a ground width of 1.2488 times the altitude in nadir. The nadir camera points down within 3.5 degrees of vertical at a heading drawn per image, at a commanded altitude of 150, 75 or 35 m with a per-image jitter of up to 6 m (3 m at 35 m, 13 m in the twin at 150 m). The oblique camera flies at 120 m with a jitter of up to 36 m, at a pitch drawn per image between 26 and 52 degrees from the vertical (S-oblique) or between 45 and 56 (S-oblique-six); Figure @fig{fig8_nadir_obliquo} shows the same settlement under both.

What the renderer does not simulate is stated so that Section 6.5 can be read against it. Frames are path-traced at 96 or 64 samples per pixel with denoising on, and written as lossless PNG. No sensor noise is added after denoising; motion blur, depth of field, rolling shutter, chromatic aberration, vignetting and lens distortion are not simulated; the frames carry no lossy compression. Training tiles are re-encoded once as JPEG at quality 92 without chroma subsampling, and every whole-frame evaluation reads the lossless frames. A real frame from the reference camera carries sensor noise, video compression and blur from exposure time and aircraft motion; none of these enters any corpus.

#### 4.2.5 Automatic annotation

Boxes are neither drawn by hand nor inferred from a segmentation pass (Figure @fig{fig5_anotacao}). For each figure the generator projects a subsample of the body and clothing vertices (about 500) and every vertex of any carried item into image space, takes the axis-aligned extent and clips it to the frame. The convention is amodal within the frame: the box covers the whole figure, including parts hidden by intervening geometry, as in the benchmark, and includes the carried item. Occlusion is evaluated separately by casting 14 rays from the camera to points on the figure; an instance whose visible fraction falls below 0.35 is discarded rather than kept as a degenerate box, as is any box outside the frame or whose clipped side is below 0.002 of the frame. Because boxes come from geometry rather than pixels, labelling does not depend on the regime. The class of a box is that of the carried item (firearm, nothing, confusable object); the three-class corpora record the item's type per instance.

### 4.3 Synthetic corpora and real data

Table @tab{corpora} lists every corpus with the counts on which Section 5 rests. Training corpora are matched by frames, 180 single-class and 200 three-class, so that no comparison between synthetic models is one of corpus size in frames. Instance counts differ nonetheless. Figures drawn per frame vary by corpus (16 to 34 at 150 m and oblique, 14 to 30 in the original 75 m corpus and its test frames, 12 to 24 at 35 m and in the twins, 10 to 26 in the pilot). The share of placed figures that survive the filter of Section 4.2.5 is about 0.70 in the nadir corpora at 150 m, 0.62 to 0.66 at 75 and 35 m and 0.34 to 0.41 in the oblique corpora, where a tilted frame covers less of the ground over which figures are drawn. S-oblique-six carries only figures carrying nothing. In the tiles the detector sees, counts differ again (4,678 boxes in the 1,106 training tiles of the corpus of record against 2,659 in its 149 training frames), because the 20 per cent overlap duplicates boxes along seams and a box with under 40 per cent of its area inside a tile is dropped.

The real data is VisDrone-DET (Section 2.2) in 5 subsets. The pedestrian and people categories are merged into one person class; the other categories and the annotated ignored regions are dropped from the labels. The validation split of 548 images is the test set of every single-class model, and no image of it enters training or selection except the selection of R (Section 4.5). R trains on 1,106 images drawn at random from the training split (18,858 persons), the number of training tiles in the corpus of record, which matches the ceiling by images, not by instances. Three slices of the training split serve Experiment B: 5 per cent (324 images), 10 per cent (647, containing the first) and a selection slice of 324 disjoint from both.

The second real test set is HERIDAL (Božić-Štulić, Marušić, & Gotovac, 2019), people on the ground in Mediterranean scrub photographed straight down from 40 to 65 m (Pyrrö, Naseri, & Jung, 2021), at an estimated 1.5 to 2.4 cm per pixel, in the version distributed under a CC BY 3.0 licence by Pyrrö (2021). It is the only public set found with person boxes on land, a declared nadir view and a GSD about two to three times finer than the reference acquisition; its scene is wilderness, not a city. Every annotated image is used, 1,647 images of 4000 by 3000 pixels with 3,229 persons, 561 of them with no person (3 of the 1,650 annotation files have no image), and the set serves only zero-shot evaluation: no image of it enters training or selection.

Held-out test frames were rendered after the models they test had been trained, and were used for neither training nor selection. They are 80 new frames continuing the seed of the 35 m corpus, 80 continuing that of its twin at 75 m and 80 that of its twin at 150 m, the three at the same indices, and 120 continuing that of the corpus of record, 20 per regime. At 75 m, 50 frames in a new realisation of the settlement carry the 2 unseen models in half the firearms, and the same 50 frames are re-rendered with every confusable object carried exactly as a weapon, every other draw unchanged.

Three further training corpora derive from existing ones by changing one setting. The hard-sun-only corpus repeats the command of the corpus of record, same settlement and scene seed, for 180 frames under the hard-sun regime alone, six times the hard-sun frames of the original; its frames differ from the per-regime test frames. Two replicas of S-oblique-six repeat its command with settlement seeds 22 and 23 instead of 21, so that the six settlements are drawn anew at the same sites. The learning-curve subsets are nested subsets of the 152 training frames of S-oblique-six with 30, 60, 90 and 120 frames, stratified by settlement, each with all the tiles of its frames (182, 341, 503 and 696 training tiles against 890) and the same validation tiles as the full corpus.

### 4.4 Model-free characterisation of the target

Three properties of the target are measured from annotations and rendered pixels alone, on the pilot corpus, the corpus of record, the 2 oblique corpora and the benchmark, and a fourth from a pretrained backbone, on the four three-class corpora (S-nadir-35, its twins at 75 and 150 m and the original 75 m corpus, S-nadir-75); all four are independent of any trained detector, and Section 5.1 reports the values.

Target size by GSD. A person seen from directly above presents the plan of the figure, not their height. For each corpus the median long side, width, height and area of the annotated box are measured at native resolution, with the share of instances whose shorter side falls below 8 pixels, one stride of the detector's finest feature map (Section 2.3), since the shorter side is what has to survive one stride.

Apparent shape by camera pitch. The height-to-width ratio of the box is measured on the same sets and, in the S-oblique corpus, split by the pitch recorded per image in bands of about 5 degrees, with figures carrying nothing measured separately because a carried item widens the box. The nadir corpora carry the generator setting rather than a per-image pitch, the benchmark records no acquisition geometry, and both benchmark splits are measured because the oblique arm is calibrated on the training split alone.

Contrast against local clutter by regime. For every annotated instance of the pilot corpus (48 frames, 8 per regime) the mean luminance inside the box, the mean of a 6 pixel ring around it and the SD of that ring are computed. The first difference is raw contrast, in digital numbers of 0 to 255; divided by the ring deviation it is contrast against local clutter. Instances within one image are not independent, so the statistic is aggregated per image and dispersion is reported between images. A contrast statistic is not a detection statistic: it yields the hypothesis that Experiment D tests.

Crop similarity by GSD. A fourth measurement asks whether crops of the 3 classes separate at each GSD without any detector trained on the corpora, on the four three-class corpora: S-nadir-35, its twins at 75 and 150 m and the original 75 m corpus, S-nadir-75 (Table @tab{corpora}). Every annotated figure of the validation split of each and, where they exist, of its 80 held-out frames is cropped with a margin of 25 per cent of the box on each side and fitted into 224 by 224 pixels. Each crop is embedded by the frozen backbone (layers 0 to 10) of the detector of Section 4.5 as pretrained on COCO (Lin et al., 2014), as the spatial mean of its last feature map (512 dimensions). Vectors are centred by the pool mean and normalised, and similarity is the cosine. Separation is read from the mean cosine between and within classes, the cosine between class centroids, the silhouette, and nearest-centroid and 5-nearest-neighbour classification under 5-fold stratified cross-validation, scored by accuracy, object rate, armed recall, d′ (Section 4.7) and the area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) for armed against object. The classification is repeated after principal component analysis (PCA) to 2 and 10 dimensions, after a supervised Fisher projection to 2 dimensions (linear discriminant analysis, LDA), and fitted on the training split and applied to the evaluation pool; intervals are 95 per cent by resampling frames. A supplement, not model-free, repeats the pipeline with the backbone of each trained three-class detector. Here d′ is computed without the log-linear correction of Section 4.7, and the detector's d′ quoted beside it is recomputed the same way.

### 4.5 Detector and training protocol

The detector is YOLO11s, the small member of the Ultralytics YOLO11 family of single-stage detectors (Jocher & Qiu, 2024), trained on one NVIDIA RTX 2060 with 6 GB. Every model starts from weights pretrained on COCO (Section 2.2), the generic initialisation of this article; the synthetic-start fine-tuning models start from S-oblique-six. Synthetic training operates on tiles cut at native scale, never on downscaled frames, because downscaling a 3840 by 2160 frame to 1536 pixels takes the median instance width reported in Section 5.1 to about 7 pixels, below one stride. The real-data models train on whole benchmark images resized to 1280 pixels on the long side, and the joint models on both kinds of input. Table @tab{params} gives the shared settings.

| Inference | confidence 0.001 and non-maximum suppression at IoU 0.7 (defaults, not tuned) for mAP; benchmark images resized to 1280 px on the long side; three-class rates at confidence 0.25 on whole frames (Section 4.7) |
| Seeds | training seed 0 with deterministic operations; seeds 1 and 2 for the 10 repeated models; settlement seed 21 for S-oblique-six, 22 and 23 for its replicas; the 1,106 images of R drawn with seed 21; the 3 benchmark slices with seed 2609 |

The selection split is the set of images on which the fitness, mAP50-95 (Section 4.7) at the validator's default thresholds, is computed at the end of each epoch. The checkpoint is saved whenever the fitness strictly exceeds its previous best, and training stops after 15 epochs without improvement; the fine-tuning and joint models of the original schedule ran their 30 epochs, so their comparison is at a fixed budget, which the schedule grid keeps; the epoch at which the best weights of each model were saved is a result and is reported in Section 5. Which split selects differs by family, and this is where validation and test have to be told apart. The synthetic models select on the tiles of the validation frames of their own corpus, a split held out from training but not from selection; the single-class ones are then tested on the benchmark, which never enters their selection, while the three-class ones are scored on that selection split and, separately, on the held-out test frames of Section 4.3. The fine-tuning and joint models select on the selection slice of Section 4.3. R selects on the benchmark's validation split, which is also its test set, over its 60 epochs without early stopping, the epoch of its best weights likewise left to Section 5: its ceiling is optimistic by selection, but R also trains on the untuned shared schedule, so the direction of the net bias in the efficacy ratios against it is not known. Three runs (the twin at 75 m with seed 0, S-nadir-35 with seed 1 and F-joint at 10 per cent) were paused and resumed from the last saved state, which restarts the draw of augmentations, so they are not bit-identical to continuous runs. The pilot used an earlier recipe: 2 classes, tiles and input of 640 pixels, batch 8 and a budget of 120 epochs cut short at 24.

Experiment A (zero-shot transfer; RQ1). R, S-nadir-150, S-nadir-150-six, S-oblique and S-oblique-six are scored on the same real test set, and every synthetic score is reported as a ratio to R. S-nadir-150-six keeps every setting and the 180 frames and spreads them over the 6 settlements, 30 each. S-oblique keeps the settlement seed, materials, regimes, class shares and annotation and moves the camera, with the settings that depend on it. These are the figure-placement filter (a square of side 0.52 of the frame width around the nadir axis, against anywhere the oblique frame covers) and the GSD (about 4.3 to 6.3 cm per pixel at the centre of the oblique frame across the pitch range, against 4.88). The weapon library also differs, the nadir corpus carrying the earlier one (Section 4.2.2). The difference between the two is read as the contribution of capture geometry with those settings. S-oblique-six combines the oblique camera with 6 settlements, a pitch band chosen to centre the shape of figures carrying nothing on the benchmark's training split, only such figures, and a camera placement that frames the settlement rather than forest or hillside. S-oblique-six against S-oblique therefore changes the pitch band, the classes and the framing along with the number of settlements, and is read as a package of factors; settlement variety on its own is isolated only in the nadir pair, S-nadir-150-six against S-nadir-150, with a single run on the six-settlement side. S-nadir-150 and S-oblique are trained with seeds 1 and 2 besides seed 0, so that the geometry comparison rests on three runs per side; S-oblique-six is also rendered with two further settlement seeds and trained on nested subsets of its frames, the subsets of 30 and 120 frames with three seeds each and those of 60 and 90 with one, every point at the same 60 epochs, so that the smaller points take fewer gradient steps. Three checks accompany the experiment. The first, scale matching, evaluates the test set at the input size (1,024 pixels, chosen before any evaluation) at which the median long side of its person boxes, as they reach the network, matches the corpus of record. The second is the ceiling check, whether R itself performs near chance, in which case the protocol rather than synthesis would be at fault. The third scores R and the four synthetic arms zero-shot on the nadir benchmark of Section 4.3, under the same protocol: one class, confidence 0.001, non-maximum suppression at IoU 0.7. Its input size follows the rule of the scale check, fixed before any evaluation from labels and image sizes alone: the multiple of 32 at which the median long side of the benchmark's person boxes (71.0 pixels at native resolution) reaches the network closest to the 20.55 pixels of the corpus of record, which is 1,152 pixels (20.45); the protocol size of 1,280 (22.72) is the secondary measure. The efficacy there is a ratio to R, trained on the urban benchmark, not to a ceiling trained on nadir imagery.

Experiment B (limited real data; RQ2). The 5 and 10 per cent slices each train 2 models for 30 epochs: F-synthetic, from S-oblique-six, and F-generic, from COCO. Both first receive the same untuned schedule. A grid of four schedules then crosses a trainable or frozen backbone (layers 0 to 10) with a learning rate of 0.002 or 0.0002, the optimiser set explicitly to what the automatic choice had selected (Table @tab{params}), so that the original schedule is one cell; every cell keeps the budget of 30 epochs. A control run with the explicit optimiser has to reproduce the original generic model at 5 per cent within one SD of its three seeds, the value reported in Section 5.3. For each start and slice the schedule is the cell whose best checkpoint has the highest mAP50-95 on the selection slice, never on the test set, and the chosen cell at 5 per cent is trained with seeds 1 and 2 where it is not the original. F-joint starts from COCO and trains for 30 epochs on the 890 training tiles of S-oblique-six with the same 324 or 647 real images. The quantity of interest is the difference at each volume between the model that uses the synthetic corpus and F-generic, each start on its chosen schedule in the grid; F-joint is not rerun under the new schedules.

Experiment C (the armed and unarmed distinction by GSD; RQ3). S-nadir-75 is scored, as 3 classes, on its selection split and on the two 75 m held-out sets. The unseen-weapon test compares recall on the 5 training firearms with recall on the 2 unseen ones in the same 50 frames under the same light; both unseen models lie inside the modelled length range of the training weapons, so the test measures the asset rather than a new size. The carried-as-weapon test pairs the 227 confusable objects matched box for box between the two renders and compares the rate at which the detector calls them armed as normally carried and when carried in the weapons' poses and angles. S-nadir-35 and its twins at 75 and 150 m, identical to it except in altitude and its jitter, separate resolution from carrying pose and from the other settings that changed since the original 75 m corpus. The 35 m model and the twin at 75 m are scored on their validation splits and replicated on the 80 held-out frames of each corpus, across 3 training seeds and at IoU 0.50, 0.60 and 0.75. The twin at 150 m, first trained with the standard recipe, is trained again with a budget of 120 epochs and a patience of 30, the larger budget fixed for it, and is scored on its validation split and on the 80 held-out frames at 150 m, which share their indices with the held-out frames at 35 and 75 m, so that d′ and the object rate can be read across the three GSDs on matched frames.

Experiment D (illumination). S-nadir-150 is evaluated per regime on the 120 held-out frames of its settlement, 20 per regime, as mAP50 on the tiles of each regime and, as a secondary measure fixed with it, recall at confidence 0.25 on whole frames. The experiment measures difficulty by regime inside the synthetic world for one model trained on all 6 regimes in equal shares, and no real image is involved. An ablation then trains a model on the hard-sun-only corpus of Section 4.3 and scores it on the same frames. Two comparisons follow, on recall at confidence 0.25: the specialist against the model of all regimes under hard sun, on the same 20 frames (D-i, descriptive), and the specialist under hard sun against the model of all regimes under overcast (D-ii, the one that decides). If the interval of D-ii lies wholly below zero and the specialist's mAP50 under hard sun stays below the overcast figure of the model of all regimes (Section 5.5), the deficit belongs to the regime; otherwise it belongs to the share of hard sun in training.

Confirmatory against exploratory. ### 4.7 Metrics, statistical analysis and pre-specified criteria

Detection is scored by mAP50 (Section 2.2) and by mAP50-95, the same quantity averaged over IoU thresholds from 0.50 to 0.95, the fitness on which every checkpoint is selected. Precision and recall are those the validator returns at the confidence that maximises F1, the harmonic mean of precision and recall, not at the 0.001 threshold used for mAP. The efficacy of a synthetic model is its mAP50 divided by that of R on the same test set, R being a ceiling for the protocol, not for VisDrone: it trains on 1,106 of the 6,471 training images under the shared schedule. Precision-recall curves at IoU 0.50 are drawn on the benchmark for R, S-oblique-six (seed 0), F-generic and F-synthetic at 10 per cent, with the F1-maximising point marked. Under the validator a detection inside one of the benchmark's ignored regions counts as a false positive; every single-class score is also recomputed under the rule of the official VisDrone toolkit, which drops any box with half or more of its area inside such a region, and at IoU 0.60 and 0.75.

The three-class models are scored on whole frames at native scale at confidence 0.25 (the default, not tuned). Predictions are matched one to one with annotated figures at IoU 0.50 without regard to class, each figure takes the class of its best-overlapping prediction, and an unmatched figure counts as not called armed. Four rates follow: the recall of armed figures, the rate at which a confusable object is called armed (the object rate), the rate at which an unarmed person carrying nothing is called armed, and the precision of the armed class (armed figures called armed over all armed predictions). Each rate is a lower bound, since a second armed box on a figure matched to another class enters only the precision. The rates give d′, the difference between the normal quantiles of the armed recall and of the rate at which the other class is called armed, which separates how well a model distinguishes 2 classes from how readily it calls a figure armed, with a log-linear correction for rates near zero or one (Hautus, 1995). The confidence threshold is swept from 0.05 to 0.95 in steps of 0.05, and each model is reported at 3 operating points: the default of 0.25, the threshold that maximises the F1 of the armed class, and the lowest threshold at which the object rate falls to 0.10 or below. No threshold, in d′ or in any of these rates, at which the capability to tell an armed figure from one carrying an object would be read as present was fixed before the results; the operating points are reported descriptively, and Section 6 reads them.

Ten models (S-nadir-150, S-oblique, S-oblique-six, its subsets of 30 and 120 frames, both fine-tuning models at 5 per cent on the original schedule, F-generic at 5 per cent on the schedule its selection slice preferred (Section 5.3), S-nadir-35 and its twin at 75 m) were trained 3 times, with seeds 0, 1 and 2 on a fixed corpus, and the range over runs is reported; S-oblique-six was also trained, with seed 0, on its two settlement-seed replicas, and every other model was trained once. Intervals on a benchmark are by resampling images: 1,000 resamples of the 548 test images of VisDrone-DET, or of the 1,647 images of HERIDAL, with replacement, seed 0, the same for every model, with the interval at the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles; efficacy, paired differences and ratios between models are computed on the same resamples, and where a side has several runs the difference is taken between the means over runs of each side. Intervals for the three-class rates and d′ are by 1,000 resamples of frames with seed 0, models at different GSDs resampled independently; intervals for the per-regime recall of Experiment D are by 2,000 resamples of frames, paired on the same frames for D-i and independent for D-ii. Two proportions are compared by a two-sided two-proportion test without correction for clustering by frame, a test that treats instances as independent although instances of one frame are not (Section 4.4); the resampling by frame that gives the intervals of the rates and of d′ is its conservative companion. The paired carried-as-weapon rates are compared by an exact McNemar test. The recall ratio of the unseen-weapon test carries an approximate 95 per cent interval that ignores the clustering of boxes within frames.

What counts as a resolvable difference was fixed before any interval was computed. Reading (a) is that the ranges of the two models over their 3 training seeds do not overlap; it requires seeds on both sides and, where one side was trained once, is recorded as where that value falls relative to the other's range. Reading (b) is that the 95 per cent interval of the paired difference excludes zero. The relative difference, as a share of the smaller value, is reported beside the absolute one, with 30 per cent as a third, descriptive reading. A difference that passes neither (a) nor (b) is reported as not resolvable. Section 5.6 applies the rule to every headline comparison.

### 4.8 Reproducibility

Every generated image records its seed, illumination regime, commanded and realised altitude and, in corpora generated after the generator began recording it, its camera pitch; the pilot corpus and the corpus of record carry the pitch setting instead. The settlement, camera and illumination of every reported image are reproducible by the authors from what the image records. The figures are reproducible only in corpora generated after the figure generator began drawing from a seeded stream: in the 3 rendered before that defect was found (the pilot corpus, the corpus of record and the original 75 m corpus) their placement and appearance cannot be regenerated exactly. Training is deterministic under a fixed seed (Section 4.5), and 10 models were repeated with 2 further seeds.

The scene generator and its code are not released, and neither the corpora nor the trained weights accompany the article, so a reader cannot check the corpora themselves. What a reader can check is each benchmark, distributed by its own authors, the published specification of the reference camera, and every parameter of Sections 4.2 to 4.7, which suffices to re-implement the protocol and to approximate the generator, every asset being public domain.
