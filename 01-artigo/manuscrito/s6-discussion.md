## Discussion

Sections 6.1 to 6.3 interpret the results against the literature, Section 6.4 develops the acquisition-conditioned capability statement and its propositions, and Sections 6.5 and 6.6 give limitations and future work.

### 6.1 Answering the research questions in light of the literature

**RQ1: transfer from synthetic imagery alone.** Synthetic-only training recovered a small fraction of what real images give on VisDrone (Table @tab{obliquo}), and camera geometry, pitch together with the settings that change with it, moved that fraction most, across training runs. Settlement variety, varied alone only at nadir, added an amount inside the range of the single-settlement runs; the larger gain of S-oblique-six came with a new pitch band, figures and camera placement, and a new draw of the settlements spread the score 1.4 times as widely as a new training run, below the factor of two pre-specified for the scene to dominate. More frames still helped at 152, but too slowly to make more imagery from the same generator a cheap route to the ceiling.

Since apparent size was matched between S-nadir-150 and VisDrone but not between the two arms, the nadir benchmark tests shape: from directly above a person is the plan of a figure, as wide as it is long, whereas VisDrone's people stand (Online Appendix, Table C2a). The reversal on HERIDAL, though not run by run and on a benchmark that also differs in scene, altitude and GSD, is consistent with the factor on VisDrone being largely the cost of a mismatch between the geometry of the corpus and that of the test imagery rather than a defect of nadir synthesis; the oblique arm, calibrated on VisDrone's training split, shows what that benchmark rewards, not how a settlement should be flown.

Our best oblique corpus, at 9.66 mAP50 points on three-run means, sits at the upper end of the 1.2 to 10.2 that game-engine imagery alone gave Kiefer et al. (2022) on the same benchmark, a comparison that holds in order of magnitude only, since their figures average ten classes over a ceiling trained on the full split; the direction agrees with the cost of removing variation in viewing angle, sun and weather reported by Liao et al. (2026).

**RQ2: the synthetic corpus once some real images exist.** It added no accuracy (Section 5.3): starting from the synthetic weights cost accuracy against a generic start at both volumes, more when each start ran on its preferred schedule and most at the smaller volume, where the pre-specified expectation placed the largest gain. The sign agrees with the negative transfer of Section 2.2 (Shen et al., 2023a, 2023b; Yim et al., 2026), and freezing the backbone, which brought render-only training close to real training for Hinterstoisser et al. (2019), lowered the synthetic start here, as Tremblay et al. (2018) found with highly randomised scenes. Whether the size, the variety or the image domain of the corpus decides the sign this design cannot say (Mensink et al., 2022).

**RQ3: the armed and unarmed distinction by GSD.** On synthetic imagery the capability was not demonstrated at any of the three GSDs. The detectors separated armed figures from figures carrying an ordinary object only partially, and the confusion was lowest at 2.44 cm per pixel and did not fall further at 1.14, on the first run and on the means of three runs (Section 5.4). At 4.88 cm per pixel the separation was weaker than at 2.44 on the same frames; Twin-150 had the larger training budget, so this difference, if anything, understates the gap due to GSD. At that GSD the outline of a firearm is not resolved (Section 4.6), so the separation rests on a cue other than its form. No threshold for calling the capability present was pre-specified (Section 4.7), so the reading rests on the descriptive operating points of Section 5.4, at none of which a model kept the object rate below 0.09 with armed recall above 0.42 (Table @tab{declaracao}). Not demonstrated, rather than absent, is the strongest claim the data support.

Which cue the detectors use is not established. Length is one candidate: at 75 m, where weapons and objects shared their carrying poses, the closed umbrella, the one object short enough to be carried like a long arm, was the object most often called armed. Carrying pose is another, since in the 35 m corpus and its twins the weapon is held forward far more often than the object (Section 4.2.2): at 4.88 cm per pixel figures carrying the pistol, about four pixels long, were called armed at a rate within the range of those carrying long arms, which neither the form nor the length of so short a firearm explains. The carried-as-weapon test removed the angle cue at 75 m only. Without a detector the classes separated less, and armed and object separated more as resolution coarsened, again consistent with a cue other than form (Section 5.1).

### 6.2 Geometry as a design variable

The design of synthetic aerial data is usually posed as a problem of scene realism. Photorealism was not varied here, so realism and geometry cannot be ranked, but the camera-geometry bundle moved transfer by a factor of four on three-run means: GSD sets the size of the target and viewpoint its shape, and the nadir shape ratio is the same at two render resolutions and at six times as many instances (Online Appendix, Table C2a), so a more photorealistic generator need not change it. Matching the acquisition geometry is thus the first candidate for a corpus designer, pending a nadir benchmark over a city; the cost of generic geometry, measured for vehicles by Liao et al. (2026), remains unmeasured here.

### 6.3 Implications for acquisition practice

Altitude is only one means of reaching a GSD, which trades resolution against coverage: from 150 m the reference camera reaches the GSD of the 35 m corpus only at 8.5 times zoom, imaging 73 times less ground per frame (Table @tab{gsdzoom}), and on synthetic frames the confusion was no lower there than at 2.44 cm per pixel. Hard sun was the weakest light on synthetic frames, and a model trained under it alone recovered most of the deficit but lost accuracy under the other regimes (Section 5.5): if that holds on real imagery, a detector meant to work under a high sun needs that light in its training set, without a corpus weighted too far toward it. Nothing here recommends acquisition over the territory (Section 4.1).

Read through the statement, the object rate is the synthetic analogue of the error placed at the ethical core in Section 3.3, a resident carrying a household object marked as armed; it bounds no field rate, having been measured on synthetic imagery, at an untuned threshold, on carrying poses set by the generator, and having moved with the realisation of the settlement. Its consequence for an operator is read through precision: with roughly a third of figures armed by construction, fewer than half of the armed calls fell on armed figures, and at a lower prevalence of armed persons the same rates would yield a larger share of false alerts. Two pre-specified tests, run on the original 75 m model only, rule out the simplest artefacts there: unseen firearm models were found as often as seen ones, and carrying objects as weapons are carried did not raise the object rate (H16, H17); neither covers the 35 m corpus and its twins, whose carrying poses differ by class (Section 6.1). Where a term of the statement is missing, the claim has not been made in a form that can be verified. The study did not examine procurement practice; the statement sets out what the measurements require, not how agencies behave.

Three propositions for administrative research follow. None was tested here; each names the design that would test it.

**Proposition 1 (untested).** Oversight reviewers given a statement with acquisition conditions classify a contested alert as inside or outside the claimed conditions more accurately than reviewers given an unconditioned claim, which lacks the information with which account-giving begins (Busuioc, 2021). A vignette experiment assigning reviewers to the two forms of claim would test it.

**Proposition 2 (untested).** Trust in confirming alerts amplifies the cost of the object rate. Officers trust algorithmic recommendations that confirm their own judgement (Selten et al., 2023), and a false armed call during an incursion against an armed group is such a recommendation, so human review is weakest where the object rate matters. A vignette experiment after Selten et al. (2023), varying whether an armed alert confirms or disconfirms the officer's prior assessment, would test it.

**Proposition 3 (untested).** Agencies whose procurement rules require the statement show fewer acquisitions of systems used outside their stated conditions. Such a rule moves scrutiny from the moment of force to the moment of procurement, as agency rules that act ahead of force, such as a requirement to report pointing a firearm at a person, have been associated with fewer officer-involved gun deaths (Jennings & Rubado, 2017); a cross-agency comparison in the manner of that study would test it.

### 6.5 Limitations

**1. Benchmarks.** VisDrone shows dense urban scenes, not a favela, from an estimated 30 to 100 m, with only about 16 per cent of its annotated training objects in bird-view images (Kiefer et al., 2021; Nguyen et al., 2026); HERIDAL shows wilderness at an estimated GSD two to three times finer than the reference acquisition, against a reference model trained on urban imagery, and its nadir runs spread too widely for the seed reading. No public benchmark has person boxes over a city at a nadir view near 5 cm per pixel.

**2. Synthetic realism.** One sensor was simulated, without noise, motion blur or lossy compression (Section 4.2.4), so synthetic rates are likely optimistic for the same GSD, and the 4K render, which pairs the GSD of the reference camera at 2× zoom with the footprint of its wide setting, is an optimistic stand-in for the reference acquisition. No real aerial benchmark found reports detection by light (Zhu et al., 2022; Wen et al., 2021; Barekatain et al., 2017).

**3. Generator rules as cues.** Figures come from six meshes per run, and weapons and objects differ by rules of the generator, not by observation of how residents carry things (Section 4.2.2); the tests of Section 5.4 neutralise the asset library and, at 75 m, the carrying angle, not length or pose.

**4. One detector, runs and schedules.** Every model is YOLO11s, and the sign of synthetic pretraining varies with detector size in the literature (Shen et al., 2023b; Yim et al., 2026). Ten models were trained three times and the rest once (Section 4.7); the settlement seed, varied for one corpus only, spread the score 1.4 times as widely as the training seed, so single-run dispersion may be understated. The learning curve covers the oblique corpus only, four schedules were tried at a fixed budget, and the joint models ran on the original schedule only. The checks of Section 5.6 are not corrected for multiplicity, and two-proportion tests ignore clustering by frame.

**5. Selection, test, the reference model and the threshold.** Three-class scores on selection splits are optimistic. R selected its checkpoint on its test split, which flatters it, on an untuned schedule, which may handicap it (Section 4.5), so the direction of the net bias in each share of R is not known; it also saw about four times as many person boxes as S-nadir-150, so part of each gap may be one of instance count. S-oblique-six is the best of four configurations on the same test set (Section 4.6). No threshold for calling the armed distinction present was pre-specified, the confidence threshold was not tuned, and the share of armed figures is a generator setting, so armed precision carries over to no population.

**6. Scope.** Positionality and sources are stated in Sections 3 and 4.1. No imagery or geometry of Jacarezinho enters the study, so the findings license no inference to informal urban verticality in general.

### 6.6 Future work

The design would be completed by further seeds for the models trained once, a settlement seed varied at nadir, a nadir learning curve, the joint models under every schedule, and zero-shot evaluation on nadir imagery of people over built ground at the target GSD (Online Appendix B5 lists the nadir sets found and why none suffices). Consented imagery of informal settlements elsewhere, as published for Kigali and Slovo Park (Gevaert et al., 2016; Sithole & Mhangara, 2025), would test generalisation to construction closer to the target than either benchmark.

What body would conduct the independent ethics review and community engagement of Section 4.1, what form consent would take under armed control, and whether meaningful consent is obtainable there remain open; the next step is the design of that process, not a refinement of the detector, and, on the administrative side, the experiments of Section 6.4.

A classifier of vertical morphology (building height, rooftop addition and material transition), the model that motivated the choice of site, would share the generator, which already records the footprint, storey count and roof height of every structure, and its domain randomisation (Prakash et al., 2019); it was not built, and no figure reported here transfers to it.
