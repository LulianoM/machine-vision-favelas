## Discussion

### 6.1 Answering the research questions in light of the literature

**RQ1: transfer from synthetic imagery alone.** Without real training images, detection on the urban benchmark reached, as means of three training runs, 2.2 per cent of the ceiling for the reference acquisition (3.1 for the best run), 8.8 per cent with the same settlement seen obliquely (9.7) and 15.3 per cent for S-oblique-six (16.7) (Section 5.2). Camera pitch moved the figure most on that benchmark: from S-nadir-150 to S-oblique by +0.0419 of mAP50 on the means of three runs per side, a factor of 4.08 (3.44 to 4.64) and 307.9 per cent, which passes all three readings of Section 4.7, the factor ranging from 2.79 to 6.011 seed by seed. Six settlements instead of one moved it less, by +0.0411 obliquely (ratio 1.74, 1.64 to 1.85; 74.0 per cent), which passes all three readings, and by +0.0054 at nadir on the mean of the three nadir runs (ratio 1.40, 1.07 to 1.50; 39.9 per cent), which passes the interval and relative readings but not the seed reading, the six-settlement value falling inside the range of the three nadir runs. Drawing the six settlements anew with two further seeds spread S-oblique-six over 1.4 times the range of its training seeds, below the factor of 2 fixed as the sign that the scene dominates, and six settlements stayed above one in all three readings (+68.7 per cent). The number of frames is operant: from 30 to 152 frames the score rose by 56.5 per cent in all three readings, and from 120 to 152 by 6.9 per cent in the interval reading only, so the curve is still rising at 152 frames, within the dispersion between training runs, by 0.0140 per doubling on a logarithmic fit. The 15.3 per cent need not be the ceiling of the method with this generator, but on that fit each further doubling of frames buys little. Apparent size was matched (301 against 300 square pixels for the median instance), and matching the median long side as well moved the nadir figure by a quarter, below the half fixed in advance as the sign of a scale confound (Sections 5.1 and 5.2).

What remains once size is matched is shape. Seen from directly above, a person is the plan of a figure, as wide as it is long, with a median height-to-width ratio of 0.98 in both nadir corpora, against 1.95 and 2.20 for the standing figures of the urban benchmark (Section 5.1). The nadir benchmark measures that reading rather than leaving it argued. On HERIDAL, people seen straight down, the order of the two geometries reversed: S-nadir-150 reached 62.7 per cent of the ceiling and S-oblique 42.1, a difference of -0.0656 that passes the interval and relative readings, though not the seed reading, the nadir runs spreading widely; with six settlements the nadir model reached 78.1 per cent, and the order held at both input sizes. The ratio of oblique to nadir, 4.08 on the urban benchmark, was 0.671 on the nadir one, so the factor on the urban benchmark is the cost of a mismatch between the geometry of the corpus and that of the test set, and the corpus whose geometry matches the test set transfers better. Three reservations remain. The oblique arm is an adaptation of the corpus to the urban benchmark, calibrated on its training split, so it says what that benchmark rewards and not how the settlement should be flown. The oblique camera changes altitude, the figure-placement filter and the GSD along with pitch (Section 4.6), so the factor belongs to the camera with the settings that depend on it. And HERIDAL is wilderness, not a city, its GSD is estimated, and its efficacies are ratios to R, trained on the urban benchmark, which scores 0.3185 there; the shares say how the two synthetic geometries rank against one real model on nadir imagery, not how close either comes to a model trained on it.

Kiefer, Ott and Zell (2022) obtained 1.2 to 10.2 points of mAP50 on the same benchmark from game-engine images alone (Section 2.2), 23.2 per cent of real training for their YOLO row. Our best oblique figures sit at the upper end of that range, 9.7 points for the mean of three runs of S-oblique-six, and just above it, 10.5 for the best run. The ratio to our own ceiling stays below theirs, on denominators that differ (1,106 images and one class against the full training split and ten classes). The direction agrees with the 30.4 points that removing variation in sun, weather and viewing angle cost Liao et al. (2026) on YOLOv11, without extending their claim, since two benchmarks that are not the target domain cannot carry a general statement about camera position; their other ablation is taken up in Section 6.2.

**RQ2: the synthetic corpus once some real images exist.** With the 5 or 10 per cent slices of real images (Section 4.3), the synthetic corpus added no accuracy in either use (Section 5.3). As pretraining under the original schedule it finished below the generic start by 0.0716 of mAP50 on the seed means at 5 per cent, 15.8 per cent, and by 0.0614, 12.1 per cent, at 10; mixed in, by 0.0637, 13.8 per cent, and by 0.0281, 5.2 per cent. Under a grid of four schedules, each start on the one its selection slice preferred, the cost grew to 0.1369, 30.2 per cent, at 5 per cent, passing all three readings, and to 0.1129, 22.2 per cent, at 10, passing the interval reading: freezing the backbone or lowering the learning rate, which might have preserved what the synthetic start had learned, lowered it in every cell, while the generic start gained from the lower rate. The expectation fixed before the run failed in sign: the difference is most negative at the smaller volume, where pretraining was expected to be worth most, and what narrows as real data grows is a cost. Under the original schedule the pretraining difference passes the seed and interval readings of Section 4.7 at 5 per cent and the interval reading alone at 10, with one run per side. The joint cost passes the interval reading at both volumes, exceeds the margin fixed in advance and at 5 per cent lies outside the generic range. None of these four differences reaches the descriptive reading of 30 per cent; under the chosen schedules the pretraining difference at 5 per cent does (Section 5.6).

The sign agrees with the negative transfer reviewed in Section 2.2, and the joint models, which keep the generic initialisation, reduce the cost without removing it. The cost is not a property of the one schedule first run, since it held under four; whether the size, the variety or the image domain of the corpus decides the sign this design cannot say.

**RQ3: the armed and unarmed distinction by GSD.** On synthetic imagery alone, the detector separated figures carrying a firearm from figures carrying an ordinary elongated object only partially at 2.44 and 1.14 cm per pixel, and at 4.88 more weakly still, by what we read as a shortcut. S-nadir-75 called 0.237 of confusable objects armed on its selection split and S-nadir-35 called 0.273, against 0.165 for the twin of its corpus rendered at 75 m with every other setting held; on 80 new frames per corpus the matched pair gave 25 against 12.5 per cent (Section 5.4). Between one object in six and one in four was called armed on the selection splits, and finer resolution did not reduce the confusion. At 4.88 cm per pixel, where the median figure of the corpus of record measures 17.6 by 17.5 pixels, the twin at 150 m, trained for longer until it passed its validity condition, separated armed figures from objects above chance on 80 held-out frames, with a d′ of 0.58 (0.38 to 0.78) against 0.97 for its twin at 75 m on the same frames, and called 0.245 of objects armed, back at the 0.253 of the 35 m model. Since the form of a firearm is argued to be lost at that GSD, the rule fixed before the run reads this separation as a shortcut, by length or silhouette, and not as a capability.

The cue the detector uses is not established, and object length is the untested alternative to the form of a firearm: the five weapons are recorded at 0.20 to 1.05 m, the nine objects at 0.88 to 2.5 m, and objects longer than 1.15 m are re-angled in every pose (Section 4.2.2). Three observations point to length. The closed umbrella, at 0.88 m short enough to be carried in the weapons' poses, is the object most often called armed in the unseen-weapon test, 0.38 against 0.10 for brooms and 0.11 for pipe. The unseen rifle and submachine gun are called armed within the range of the seen long arms, while the pistol sits at 0.20. And at 150 m the figures carrying the 0.20 m pistol were called armed at 0.50, within the range of the long arms, which the form of so short a firearm at that GSD cannot explain.

Without a detector the classes did not separate (Section 5.1): the co-similarity matrices of a frozen generic extractor were flat at every GSD, and the AUC of armed against object was 0.525, 0.576 and 0.654 at 1.14, 2.44 and 4.88 cm per pixel, the last on the fewest crops. A nearest-centroid rule called 0.332 and 0.253 of objects armed under cross-validation at the two finer distances and, fitted on the training split, 0.331 and 0.231 on the selection splits against the detector's 0.273 and 0.165. Its d′, 0.23 to 0.46, stayed below the detector's 0.67 to 0.90 on the selection splits, both uncorrected. PCA to 2 or 10 dimensions and LDA did not exceed the detector, and the pooled vector of the trained backbone was as flat as the generic one. A similarity device therefore does not replace the detector. The one structure the generic space holds opposes figures carrying nothing to figures carrying anything, the same reading as the length cue above, and Section 6.6 takes up the detect-then-classify alternative.

Which differences are resolvable, by the criteria of Section 4.7, decides what the rates show. The object rate of S-nadir-35 against its twin passes all three readings on the new frames, +0.087 and 64.9 per cent on the seed means with non-overlapping ranges, and the interval and relative readings on the selection splits, where the ranges overlap. The rates for armed and unarmed persons are resolvable on neither set, the comparison with the original S-nadir-75 reverses between an IoU of 0.50 and 0.60, an artefact of annotation by the same criterion, and the difference between S-nadir-75 and the twin at 75 m is not supported (p = 0.19). None of the unresolved comparisons changes the answer: d′ against confusable objects lies between 0.77 and 0.97 across models and sets at 2.44 and 1.14 cm per pixel for the first run of each, and between 0.58 and 0.75 at 4.88, with the log-linear correction, no threshold for calling the capability present was fixed in advance, and what the models share is the absence of a demonstrated capability, not a capability with errors of different size.

### 6.2 GSD and viewpoint against photorealism

The measurements of Section 5.1 reframe a design problem usually posed as one of scene realism, on the assumption that a corpus which looks more like a favela transfers better. GSD sets the size of the target (Table @tab{gsd}), and altitude is only one means of reaching a GSD. At its wide setting the reference camera gives 9.71 cm per pixel at 150 m; from the same height it reaches 4.88 at 2.0 times zoom, 2.44 at 4.0 times and 1.14 at 8.5 times of its 30 times optical range, its footprint shrinking to 22 by 12 m at the last, 73 times less area (Section 4.2.4, Table @tab{gsdzoom}). The scale-matched control of Section 5.2 shows that what still differs at a matched long side is not scale.

Viewpoint sets the shape. The ratio of 0.98 is stable across a factor of two in render resolution and of six in instance count, and it is a property of where the camera is placed rather than of how the scene is built: a generator could be made arbitrarily more photorealistic without moving it, and no appearance-based measure of realism detects it. The geometry check of Section 5.2 is the evidence that it matters, now measured in both directions: with everything but the camera and the settings that depend on it held, the ratio moved from 0.98 to 1.46 and mAP50 on the urban benchmark rose by a factor of 4.08 over three runs per side, while on the nadir benchmark the nadir corpus led (Section 6.1). What transfers is the geometry that matches the test set, and the corpus that should be built is the one whose geometry matches the acquisition it will be used on. Neither variable is photorealism, and the second is the cheaper to manipulate; what both leave unmeasured is the cost of the generic geometry itself, which Liao et al. (2026) put at 27.0 points for their object class and which the protocol of Section 4.1 kept us from measuring for this one.

### 6.3 Implications for acquisition practice

Two measurements bear on illumination, both on synthetic imagery (Sections 5.1 and 5.5). Contrast against local clutter was highest under overcast and lowest under hard sun and rain, only that gap approaching one standard deviation between images, and on 120 new frames hard sun was the weakest regime for S-nadir-150, eight points of mAP50 below the next, and overcast the strongest, level with low sun. The condition fixed in advance for withdrawing the proposed mechanism, cast shadow from rooftop objects filling the background under a hard sun (Dare, 2005), was not met, but low sun, whose shadows are as sharp, scored level with overcast. The ablation then located the deficit in the training corpus rather than in the light: a model trained under hard sun alone, on six times as many hard-sun frames, gained 0.0818 of recall under hard sun and was no longer resolvably below the model of all regimes under overcast (-0.0572, -0.1252 to +0.0067), its mAP50 staying 6.5 per cent lower. By the rule fixed before the run, the difficulty under hard sun is a matter of its share of training, not of the regime. No real aerial benchmark isolates the contrast: VisDrone has no illumination label (Zhu et al., 2022), DroneCrowd compares light only for counting (Wen et al., 2021), and Okutama-Action reports no results by condition (Barekatain et al., 2017). The evidence licenses a conditional about training rather than about flying: if the difficulty measured under hard sun holds on real imagery of a roofscape of this kind, a detector meant to work under a high sun needs enough hard-sun imagery in its training set, and the choice of light becomes a question of which light the corpus covers rather than of when to fly.

Aerial survey specifications ask for cloud-free imagery and a solar elevation of at least 30 degrees (National Geodetic Survey, 2002) or 15 (RICS, 2021), selecting the regime that a detector trained on all six in equal shares found weakest here, hard sun at 46 to 74 degrees, and excluding one of the strongest, low sun at 9 to 25. Drone-mapping guidance prefers overcast light for three-dimensional reconstruction and full sun for orthomosaics, because it permits fast shutter speeds (Maes, 2025; Pix4D, n.d., 2022; Leitão et al., 2016; GeoCue, 2025), on a mixed empirical record (Dandois et al., 2015; Slade et al., 2025). For person detection the results favour neither light as such: imagery flown under a high sun, as the specifications ask, calls for a training corpus weighted toward that light, and the specialist's losses under the other regimes, largest under haze, show the cost of weighting it too far. Overcast flying would keep the advantage of a corpus balanced across regimes at the cost the guidance names, a slower shutter or higher gain, not measured here.

Three caveats bound the conditional. Rain is a training regime here, not a flying one: in Brazil operation under precipitation is excluded in any case (Departamento de Controle do Espaço Aéreo [DECEA], 2026), and any flight under cloud has to keep the cloud base compatible with visual meteorological conditions. Operations that answer to events rather than to a schedule, such as search and rescue (Broyles et al., 2022), cannot choose their light, and for them the finding says directly what the conditional says for all: the robustness a detector needs comes from the light its training covers. One ablation, on one model and synthetic frames, carries this reading. The implication concerns how a roofscape of this kind would be surveyed, on synthetic evidence and one detector; nothing in this article recommends acquisition over the territory (Section 6.4).

### 6.4 Dual use: the false-alarm rate as an estimate of harm

The object rate reported in Section 5.4 is the synthetic estimate, unmeasured on real imagery, of how often a system of this kind would mark a resident carrying a household object as armed, the error that Section 3.3 places at the ethical core. It is a lower bound (Section 4.7), and an operator would also see as alerts the armed boxes it leaves out. The threshold, 0.25, was not tuned, and the rates vary with the realisation of the settlement.

Two tests fixed in advance check whether the rate is an artefact of the generator (Section 5.4). Recall on firearm models the detector had never seen was 0.466 against 0.400 on the models it was trained on, a ratio of 1.16 against the withdrawal threshold of 0.80, with the pistol counted or set aside, so what it separated is not the asset library. Carrying every confusable object exactly as a weapon is carried left the object rate unchanged, 0.137 against 0.154 (exact McNemar test, p = 0.56). The generalisation that passes the first test, to length and silhouette rather than to the asset, is plausibly the one that produces the confusion.

The position this leaves is specific. Measured at 2.44 and 1.14 cm per pixel on synthetic imagery, the capability carries a false-alarm rate against ordinary objects that finer resolution did not reduce. At 4.88 cm per pixel what remains is a weaker separation read as a shortcut, with an object rate of 0.245 on held-out frames, close to the 0.253 at 1.14 and above the 0.125 at 2.44: a system flown at the reference acquisition would mark ordinary objects as armed about as often as one flown low, on a cue other than the firearm. The condition under which the 75 m figure itself would be withdrawn is stated above so that others can apply it.

### 6.5 Limitations

**1. The benchmarks.** VisDrone records dense urban scenes from uncontrolled acquisition; it is not a favela, and a transfer figure on it says nothing about performance over Jacarezinho. It was captured at an estimated 30 to 100 m with the camera mostly tilted: in its training split only about 16 per cent of the annotated objects lie in bird-view images, against 84 per cent in images with an acute viewing angle (Kiefer, Messmer, & Zell, 2021; altitude range after Nguyen et al., 2026). That the near-zero zero-shot scores of the nadir models on it measure a change of viewing geometry as much as a change of scene is now a result: on HERIDAL, nadir imagery of people on land, the same models reached 62.7 per cent of R and led the oblique ones (Section 5.2). HERIDAL has its own limits, fixed before it was used: it shows wilderness, not a city, and its GSD of 1.5 to 2.4 cm per pixel is estimated from its altitude and camera, finer than the reference acquisition; its ceiling is R, trained on the urban benchmark; and the nadir runs on it spread widely enough that the comparison with the oblique arm does not pass the seed reading. No public aerial benchmark with bounding boxes of people on land reproduces a nadir view at 150 m and about 5 cm per pixel over a city. The Stanford Drone Dataset, nadir at about 80 m over a university campus (Robicquet, Sadeghian, Alahi, & Savarese, 2016), and SeaDronesSee, whose per-frame altitude and gimbal pitch allow nadir frames between 100 and 260 m to be selected although its people are in the water (Varga, Kiefer, Messmer, & Zell, 2022), were not used.

**2. Synthetic realism.** One sensor was simulated, at two render resolutions, and the render at 4K is easier than a single frame from the camera it reproduces. The generator models geometry, materials, illumination and the optics of the camera; frames are stored losslessly as PNG and training tiles re-encoded once as JPEG at quality 92, and no sensor noise, motion blur, depth of field, rolling shutter or chromatic aberration is modelled (Section 4.2.4). The cost of the generic geometry is quoted rather than measured.

**3. Figure diversity and the invariant generator.** The human figures come from a pool of six rigged meshes per rendering run, recoloured, rescaled and posed per instance, and a carried item takes an orientation set in the frame of the body, so it does not always sit in the grip, and five weapon models stand against nine objects (Section 4.2.2).

**4. Runs and seeds.** Ten models were trained three times with training seeds on a fixed corpus, among them both arms of the nadir-to-oblique comparison, whose factor ranged from 2.79 to 6.011 seed by seed around 4.08 on the means; every other model was trained once, among them the six-settlement nadir model, the intermediate points of the learning curve, the hard-sun-only model, the twin at 150 m and most cells of the schedule grid. The settlement seed was varied for S-oblique-six alone, over three seeds, and its range was 1.4 times that of the training seeds, so the dispersion of the other models is that of training only and may understate the dispersion due to the scene. Every benchmark figure carries a 95 per cent interval by resampling images (Section 5).

**5. The fine-tuning configuration.** The sign of Experiment B held under four schedules, a trainable or frozen backbone at a learning rate of 0.002 or 0.0002, each start on the one its selection slice preferred (Section 5.3). The grid is a small search, not a tuning: the budget of 30 epochs was not varied, and the joint models were trained under the original schedule only.

**6. Selection against test.** The three-class models are scored on the split that selected their checkpoint (Section 4.5), except on the 80 new frames of the matched pair and of the twin at 150 m and the 50 of the unseen-weapon test. Since no benchmark of the kind Section 2.3 looked for exists, those scores are ceilings rather than evidence of transfer. The ceiling R itself selected its checkpoint on the benchmark split it is scored on, so every efficacy is a fraction of a slightly optimistic denominator, and the three-class rates are reported at an untuned confidence of 0.25 (Section 5.4 gives the full sweep).

**7. Corpus size.** The learning curve was run on S-oblique-six only, with the smaller points trained for the same 60 epochs and therefore fewer gradient steps; whether the nadir corpus, near the floor on the urban benchmark, behaves alike is not known. The pilot on 40 scenes, which failed on its own domain, does not isolate between its two candidate causes, the small number of frames and two thirds of instances falling below one stride.

**8. Positionality.** The study is written from outside the territory and from a security framing (Section 4.1); no resident testimony was collected, so the perspective of the people such a system would observe is absent.

**9. One site, one city.** The study concerns one favela in one city, and nothing in it licenses an inference from Jacarezinho to informal urban verticality in general; the acquisition limits rest on GSD and on generic synthetic settlements rather than on properties of Jacarezinho, which is the sense in which the title speaks of dense favelas.

**10. Cues other than the weapon.** Every firearm shares the colour and roughness palette of the confusable objects, but the objects differ from the weapons in length and in how long items are angled (Section 4.2.2). A detector may therefore separate the classes by length or silhouette rather than by the form of a firearm; the unseen-weapon test and the crop-similarity analysis bear on this without excluding it (Sections 5.1 and 5.4), and at 4.88 cm per pixel the separation measured is read as such a cue by a rule fixed in advance, not shown to be one.

**11. Sources.** The contextual material of Section 3 relies on journalistic and institutional reporting for recent events, and the population figures for Jacarezinho diverge by tens of thousands between sources, with no independent means of resolution here.

### 6.6 Future work

The design would be completed by further training seeds for the models trained once, the settlement seed varied for the nadir corpus, a learning curve over the nadir corpus, and the joint models under the schedules of the grid. The inversion measured on HERIDAL concerns wilderness at a finer GSD than the reference acquisition; a zero-shot evaluation on the nadir frames of SeaDronesSee, on the Stanford Drone Dataset and on any nadir benchmark of people in a city would test whether it holds over built ground and at the target GSD. The aerial crowd images of DLR-ACD, nadir at 4.5 to 15 cm per pixel but annotated with points, allow only a counting-style check (Bahmanyar, Vig, & Reinartz, 2019). Validation against consented imagery of informal settlements elsewhere, of the kind already published for Kigali and Slovo Park (Gevaert et al., 2016; Sithole & Mhangara, 2025), would test generalisation to dense, sloped and irregular construction on a scene type closer to the target than the benchmark offers.

The armed class need not be decided by a three-class detection head. A two-stage alternative would detect the person first and then classify the crop by its similarity to class prototypes, so that the decision rests on a distance that can be inspected. Section 5.1 shows what that design cannot rest on: the pooled vector of a crop, generic or from the trained backbone, does not separate armed figures from figures carrying an object at any of the three GSDs. If the detector holds the information, it holds it locally, at the position and scale of the figure, so the second stage would need local features read from the detector's own map, which has not been tested.

Any imagery of the territory lies on the other side of a process that has not taken place: independent ethics review and community engagement outside the police institution (Section 4.1). What body would conduct it, what form consent would take in a territory under armed control, and whether meaningful consent is obtainable there are questions this study leaves open, and the next step is the design of that process, not a refinement of the detector.

The model of vertical morphology that motivated the choice of site, a classifier of building height, rooftop addition and material transition over a domain-randomised synthetic pipeline in the manner of Prakash et al. (2019), shares the generator, the annotation procedure, the randomisation and the scale analysis of this article. The generator already records, for every structure it builds, the footprint, the storey count and the roof height (Section 4.2.1). It was not built, and it has no evaluation set at this GSD, which is itself a finding; the two tasks share a pipeline and not an object class, so no figure reported here transfers to it.
