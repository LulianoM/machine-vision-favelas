## Results: A Proposed Machine Vision Model and Synthetic-Training Strategy

Building on the theoretical and methodological groundwork above, this study proposes, at a conceptual, non-deployed level, a machine vision model designed to classify and represent the **vertical structural morphology** of Jacarezinho, with three components:

- **A structural classification backbone**, adapted from CNN-based informal-settlement detection architectures in the literature (Section 4), retrained to distinguish between building height categories, informal rooftop additions, and material transitions (a common visual proxy for informal vertical expansion), rather than the conventional binary informal/formal footprint classification.
- **A domain-randomized synthetic training pipeline**, in which procedurally generated 3D scenes approximate the generic morphological patterns of vertically dense informal housing (irregular building heights, narrow inter-building gaps, mixed rooftop materials, informal cabling and water tanks) with randomized textures, lighting, and camera viewpoints, following the structured domain randomization approach of Prakash et al. (2019). This design choice is a direct response to the ethical and access constraints identified in Sections 2 and 5: it allows model pretraining without requiring extensive, consent-fraught real-world image capture inside the community itself.
- **A limited, consent-bounded real-data fine-tuning stage**, using only imagery that meets the ethical protocol in Section 5 (no individual identification, no address-level publication), consistent with sim-to-real literature showing that even a modest volume of real fine-tuning data substantially improves generalization from synthetic-only training.

At this stage, the study's result is the **conceptual architecture and training strategy** described above, together with an explicit account of what would be required (technically and, especially, ethically) to move from this proposal to a validated, field-tested system. The study does not produce, and does not claim to produce, a deployed or operationally validated targeting tool; doing so responsibly would require independent ethical review, meaningful community consultation, and a governance framework for use limitation that falls outside the scope of a single research article.
