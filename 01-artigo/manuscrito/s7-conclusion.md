## Conclusion

Trained on synthetic imagery of a dense settlement alone, a detector recovers a small fraction of the person-detection accuracy that real training images give on a public urban aerial benchmark. Averaged over three training runs, it recovers 2.2 per cent of the ceiling for the reference acquisition, nadir at 150 m, 8.8 per cent with the same settlement rendered from an oblique camera, and 15.3 per cent for the best oblique configuration. Of the parameters RQ1 names, camera geometry (pitch, with the altitude, figure-placement filter and GSD that change with it) moves that fraction by the largest factor, consistent with the apparent shape of the target rather than its size, which was matched: the oblique corpus scores 4.08 times (3.44 to 4.64) the nadir one there, and on a nadir benchmark the order reverses, the nadir corpus reaching 62.7 per cent of the reference model's score there against 42.1, a difference that passes the interval and relative readings but not the seed reading, so what transfers is the geometry that matches the test set. Settlement variety moves the fraction by a smaller factor, 1.74 obliquely, where the pitch band, the figures and the camera placement changed with it, and 1.40 at nadir, inside the range of the nadir training runs; a new draw of the settlements spreads it 1.4 times as widely as a new training run, below the factor of two pre-specified as the sign that the scene dominates; and more frames still raise it at 152, within the dispersion between runs. On synthetic frames, detection was weakest under hard sun and, on mAP50, strongest under overcast, level with low sun, and a model trained under hard sun alone was no longer resolvably below the overcast figure in recall, its mAP50 staying 6.5 per cent lower, so the deficit was not shown to belong to the light and is read, by the rule pre-specified for that comparison, as a matter of its share of training, a result that stays conditional until it is tested on real imagery.

Once 324 or 647 real training images are available, the synthetic corpus does not add accuracy, whether used as pretraining or mixed into the training set. A generic initialisation given the same real images finished above the synthetic start by 0.0716 of mAP50 on the seed mean at 5 per cent and by 0.0614 at 10 per cent, and above the joint models by 0.0637 and 0.0281, differences that pass the interval reading, the first also the seed reading, none the 30 per cent reading. Under four fine-tuning schedules, each start on the one it preferred, the cost of the synthetic start was larger still, 30.2 per cent at 5 per cent and 22.2 at 10. What narrows as real data grows is a cost, and it does not belong to the schedule.

On synthetic imagery alone, a detector separates a figure carrying a firearm from one carrying an everyday elongated object only partially at 1.14 and 2.44 cm per pixel. At those GSDs it calls between one object in six and somewhat more than one in four armed on the selection splits (0.165 to 0.273), and 25.3 against 12.5 per cent on held-out frames for the 35 m corpus and its twin at 75 m, so the finer GSD did not reduce that rate. At 4.88 a model that passed its validity condition separated them more weakly (d′ 0.58 against 0.97 at 2.44 on the same frames, both with the log-linear correction) and called 24.5 per cent of objects armed, so across the three GSDs the rate was lowest at the middle one and did not fall further at the finest; since the form of a firearm is argued to be lost at 4.88, that separation is read, by the rule pre-specified for it, as a shortcut rather than as a capability. No threshold for calling the capability present was fixed in advance, so that it was not demonstrated is a reading of the operating points rather than the outcome of a test: at the default confidence the armed class was called with a precision of 0.37 to 0.46, and bringing the rate at which objects are called armed to 0.10 or below left an armed recall of at most 0.42 in any model on any set. The cue may be the length of what is carried rather than the form of a firearm, the similarity devices tried without a detector separated the classes more weakly than it did, and the distinction has not been tested on real imagery.

A claim that a system can tell an armed person from an unarmed one from the air is, at the altitudes and sensors described here, a claim that can be checked before it is believed, and this article declines to make it. Stating the conditions under which a capability has not been demonstrated is a contribution of the same kind as demonstrating it, and in this domain the more useful of the two. What remains open is not primarily technical: a system that can tell an armed person from an unarmed one and a system that is safe to point at Jacarezinho are different objects, and nothing in the engineering reported here decides the second. That decision belongs to a process that includes the residents of the territory, and it has not taken place.

## Declarations

**Ethics.** The study involved no human participants. No imagery of residents or of the territory of Jacarezinho was collected for this study, none enters the generator, the corpora or any measurement reported here, and no address-level coordinates are published; the photogrammetric reconstruction used in earlier exploratory work (Section 4.8) is excluded from the study by the constraints of Section 4.1. The only real data are two public benchmarks, VisDrone, distributed by its authors (Zhu et al., 2022), and HERIDAL (Božić-Štulić, Marušić, & Gotovac, 2019), in the version distributed by Pyrrö (2021) under a CC BY 3.0 licence; the synthetic corpora are rendered from procedurally generated scenes and public-domain assets, and the human figures in them are rendered meshes, so the synthetic data contain no person. The study does not deliver an operational tool. Any future field data would be conditional on an independent ethics review and on community engagement external to the police institution (Section 4.1).
