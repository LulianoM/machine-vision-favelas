## Conclusion

This article asked what a claim that an aerial detector can tell an armed person from an unarmed one must state for a public body to hold it to account, and measured, on synthetic imagery of a dense informal settlement, the two capabilities that supply its empirical terms.

**RQ1.** Trained on synthetic imagery alone, detectors recovered 2.2 per cent of the real-data ceiling on an urban, mostly oblique benchmark at the reference acquisition and 15.3 per cent in the best oblique configuration (means of three runs); camera geometry moved that share most, and on a nadir benchmark the order of the two geometries reversed, though not run by run. **RQ2.** Once 324 or 647 real images existed, the synthetic corpus lowered accuracy, as pretraining under every schedule tried and, by smaller margins, mixed into training. **RQ3.** On synthetic held-out frames, ordinary elongated objects were called armed at 24.5, 12.5 and 25.3 per cent at 4.88, 2.44 and 1.14 cm per pixel, so the confusion fell from the coarsest to the middle resolution and rose again at the finest.

These answers hold for synthetic corpora, one simulated sensor and two public benchmarks that are not the target territory, with many models trained once (Section 6.5). They fill the terms of the acquisition-conditioned capability statement (Sections 2.4 and 6.4), an untested conceptual proposal that gives a public body the information with which account-giving begins (Busuioc, 2021). No threshold for calling the armed and unarmed capability present was pre-specified, and on the descriptive operating points of Section 5.4 the capability was not demonstrated at any acquisition studied: in the sweep at 2.44 and 1.14 cm per pixel no model brought the object rate below 0.09 while keeping armed recall above 0.42, and at the default threshold 0.37 to 0.43 of armed calls on held-out frames fell on armed figures; this evidence supports no claim to detect armed persons from the air.

A system that can tell an armed person from an unarmed one and a system that is safe to point at Jacarezinho are different objects, and nothing in the engineering reported here decides the second. That decision belongs to a process that includes the residents of the territory, and that process has not taken place.
